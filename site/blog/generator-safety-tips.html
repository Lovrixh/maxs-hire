Generator Safety: Fumes, Rain and Placement

Generators are safe when used properly and dangerous when they're not. These are the basics that keep everyone safe.

Run it outdoors, always

Never run a generator inside a house, garage, tent or any enclosed space. The exhaust contains carbon monoxide, which you cannot see or smell and which can kill. Outdoors, well ventilated, exhaust pointed away from people and air intakes. This is the rule that matters most.

Keep it dry

Position it on stable, dry ground and keep it out of standing water and heavy rain. If it's wet weather, it needs cover that still lets it breathe and vent. Never touch a generator or leads with wet hands.

Protect people from shock

Use RCD-protected distribution and correctly rated, tested leads. An RCD cuts power instantly on an earth fault, which is exactly what you want around a crowd or a crew. Everything we hire is tested and tagged.

Don't overload it

Size with headroom so it isn't maxed out. Overloading trips the unit and stresses your gear. Our calculator builds in a safety margin.

Refuel safely

Turn it off and let it cool before refuelling. Keep fuel away from the hot exhaust. On operated hires we handle this for you.

FAQs

Can I run a generator indoors or in a garage?

No. Never run a generator in any enclosed space. The exhaust contains carbon monoxide, which is deadly and undetectable. Always run it outdoors in a well-ventilated spot.

Is it safe to run a generator in the rain?

It can be, with the right cover that keeps it dry while still venting, on stable dry ground, and with RCD protection. Never operate it in standing water or with wet hands. Ask us and we'll set it up safely.
