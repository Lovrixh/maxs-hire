3-Phase vs Single-Phase Generators Explained

If you've been asked whether you need single-phase or three-phase, here's the plain-English version, without the electrical lecture.

Single-phase

This is normal household and small-business power, the standard 10A and 15A plugs you know. Lights, coffee machines, tills, tools, most event and home gear all run single-phase. Our Honda inverters (2.2 to 7 kVA) are single-phase.

Three-phase

Bigger, heavier equipment, industrial motors, large HVAC, some commercial kitchen and construction gear, needs three-phase power (usually 415V). It delivers more power more smoothly for heavy loads. Our Denyo 60 kVA provides both three-phase and single-phase.

How do I know which I need?

  • Check the plug and the equipment plate. Three-phase plugs are larger with four or five pins (like a 32A or 63A lead).
  • If it's normal household plugs, you're single-phase.
  • Still unsure? Send us a photo of the plug or the equipment label and we'll tell you.

Big three-phase jobs

The 60 kVA covers most three-phase work in-house. For larger loads we arrange ultra-silent sets from 120 to 300 kVA through our network.

FAQs

Do I need single-phase or three-phase?

Most homes, events and small businesses only need single-phase. Three-phase is for heavy industrial and commercial equipment. Check the plug, or send us a photo and we'll confirm.

Can one generator do both?

Yes. Our 60 kVA Denyo provides three-phase and single-phase outlets at once, so you can run mixed loads off one machine.
